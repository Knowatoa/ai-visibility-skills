# Example: planned outline vs. what was said on stage

Talk: "ARE We F*cked? An Anti-AI-Doomer's Perspective." 40-minute meetup slot. The outline was written first, then the speaker rehearsed it and recorded a transcript. The second half below is what the transcript showed.

## Spine: planned vs. spoken

**Planned (block 4, 11 min):** four pattern/anti-pattern pairs, eight named things:
momentum / arguing · right-size the workspace / the everything-chat · make them compete · delegate and walk away / grinding to the limit.

**Spoken (14:15–36:00, 22 min):** one noun, three moves:

> I want you to think about it in terms of **jobs**.
> One, we own our most important job. Two, for everything else, AI and skills. Three, AIs never get tired, so I want them working off the clock.

Only one of the eight planned names made it to the stage, and it got stronger. It became the test for which job is yours:

> I found myself constantly arguing with the AI about it… that made me realize it wasn't actually a job I wanted to give up.

**Lesson:** ask for the organizing word first. "Jobs" was the speaker's native frame. It appears nowhere in the planned outline.

## Proof: lived vs. secondhand

| Planned proof | Tag | On stage? |
|---|---|---|
| A stranger on X wants to sue an AI lab after grinding usage limits into a hospital stay | secondhand | Dropped |
| Multi-model "arena" in a coding tool | lived but technical | Dropped |
| "Get three drafts from three models" (CEO translation) | invented | Dropped |
| — | lived, that week | **New:** fired off a coding agent from a phone at lunch, done by the time he got home |
| — | lived, that week | **New:** a cloud assistant reads the day's meeting transcripts every evening for missed IOUs |
| — | lived, that day | **New:** a friend's rehearsal feedback six hours earlier, fed to Claude to "make an emotionless decision" |

**Lesson:** tag proof. Leave a slot for this week's story. The speaker will bring a fresher one than any outline.

## Payoffs: deferred vs. local

**Planned:** block 2 names four fears with "no proof by design," and the payoffs are spread across blocks 3, 4, 6, and 7, with a promise that "one has teeth."

**Spoken (04:30–11:00):** each fear was answered on the spot, fear then reality. Adoption stats for "AI will take my job." A Jira→Linear tweet pair for "SaaS is dead." A click-rate study for "SEO is dead." The promise was never said. The planned waffle callback in block 6 never happened.

The only long-range callback that did happen was the title:

> The title of the talk was "are we F'd?" I don't think so. I think we're just getting started.

**Lesson:** pay off in the same block or the next one. Allow one long-range callback, and the title is the natural one.

## Things the outline didn't plan that the speaker did anyway

- An agenda and three takeaway lines in the first minute: *trust your expertise / do more with AI / skills are the new currency.* The outline's "What the audience gets" said something else ("two moments: momentum and arguing"), and the speaker didn't use it.
- Talking to the room in front of him (technologists) instead of the target room the outline was written for (CEOs). Every CEO variant in the outline went unused.

## Timing

Planned 36 minutes plus a Q&A buffer. Spoken: 40:00 to "Questions," no buffer. The fears block doubled, and the core block grew from 16 to 22 minutes once the stories were first-hand.

**Lesson:** plan to about 85% of the slot. The core block is the one that grows.

## A block written to the rules

> **Own your most important job** (6 min)
> - **Point:** The job you keep arguing with the AI about is the one you own. Hand it the parts, never the whole.
> - **Proof (lived):** AI drafted support replies; I rewrote every one, which is how I knew customer conversations are my job. Now Claude processes the interview transcript, and I decide what to do next.
> - **Serves:** audience: the Monday test.
